# Neurobiology and EEG Biomarkers in Epilepsy and Seizure Disorders

## 1. Neurobiological Basis of Epileptogenesis

Epilepsy is one of the most prevalent neurological disorders worldwide, characterized by an enduring predisposition to generate spontaneous, recurrent epileptic seizures. At the cellular and network level, seizures arise from:
1. **Excitation-Inhibition (E/I) Imbalance:** Pathological disruption of the balance between excitatory glutamatergic neurotransmission (NMDA, AMPA receptors) and inhibitory GABAergic neurotransmission ($GABA_A, GABA_B$ receptors).
2. **Channelopathies:** Genetic mutations in voltage-gated ion channels ($Na_V1.1$ encoded by *SCN1A*, $K_V7.2$ encoded by *KCNQ2*, $Ca_V2.1$ encoded by *CACNA1A*) causing neuronal hyperexcitability.
3. **Network Hypersynchrony:** Pathological recruitment of large neuronal populations firing paroxysmal, phase-locked action potentials.

## 2. Clinical Seizure Classification (ILAE 2017 Operational Framework)

The International League Against Epilepsy (ILAE) classifies seizures by their onset:
1. **Focal Onset Seizures:** Originating within neural networks limited to one cerebral hemisphere.
   * *Focal Aware Seizures (FAS)*: Consciousness remains intact.
   * *Focal Impaired Awareness Seizures (FIAS)*: Consciousness is compromised or lost.
   * *Focal to Bilateral Tonic-Clonic (FBTCS)*: Secondary generalization across both hemispheres.
2. **Generalized Onset Seizures:** Originating at some point within and rapidly engaging bilaterally distributed networks.
   * *Motor:* Generalized Tonic-Clonic (GTC), Atonic (drop attacks), Myoclonic.
   * *Non-Motor:* Absence seizures (typical, atypical).
3. **Unknown Onset Seizures:** When onset cannot be determined due to missing initial clinical/EEG data.

## 3. Four Temporal Phases of Epilepsy

Electrophysiologically, epilepsy is partitioned into four distinct temporal states:

```
[ Inter-ictal ] ───> [ Pre-ictal Window ] ───> [ Ictal (Seizure) ] ───> [ Post-ictal ]
  • Normal/Slowing     • Nonlinear changes       • Paroxysmal discharge   • Post-ictal exhaustion
  • Occasional IEDs    • Phase-amplitude coupling • High amplitude spikes   • Delta slowing
  • High HFO density   • 10 - 60 min before onset • Tonic/clonic patterns  • Voltage suppression
```

1. **Inter-ictal State:** The baseline period between seizures (typically $>99\%$ of recording time). Contains transient pathological events (IEDs).
2. **Pre-ictal State:** The physiological transition phase preceding seizure onset. Characterized by subtle changes in dynamical entropy, spatial correlation, and phase synchronization occurring minutes to hours prior to clinical symptoms. This is the operational target for **Seizure Prediction**.
3. **Ictal State:** The duration of the seizure itself, marked by sustained, rhythmic, hypersynchronous paroxysmal discharges across scalp/intracranial electrodes. Target for **Seizure Detection**.
4. **Post-ictal State:** The immediate aftermath of seizure termination, characterized by generalized voltage attenuation (post-ictal suppression) or diffuse polymorphic delta slowing ($0.5 - 3\text{ Hz}$).

## 4. Key EEG Biomarkers

### 4.1 Interictal Epileptiform Discharges (IEDs)
* **Morphology & Definitions:**
  * **Spikes:** Paroxysmal transients with pointed peak, duration $20 - 70\text{ ms}$, distinctly standing out from background activity.
  * **Sharp Waves:** Transients similar to spikes but lasting $70 - 200\text{ ms}$.
  * **Spike-and-Wave Complexes:** A spike immediately followed by a slow wave ($200 - 500\text{ ms}$).
* **Diagnostic Specificity:**
  * **3-Hz Generalized Spike-and-Wave:** Pathognomonic hallmark of **Childhood Absence Epilepsy**, maximal over bilateral frontal channels (`F3`, `F4`, `Fz`).
  * **Temporal Sharp Waves:** Hallmark of **Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (TLE)**, localized to anterior/mid-temporal electrodes (`F7/T3` or `F8/T4`, `T1/T2`).
  * **Rolandic Spikes:** Centro-temporal sharp waves with high amplitude, characteristic of **Self-limited (Benign) Rolandic Epilepsy of Childhood**.
  * **Hypsarrhythmia:** High-voltage, chaotic, disorganized slow background with multifocal spikes, pathognomonic of **West Syndrome (Infantile Spasms)**.

### 4.2 High-Frequency Oscillations (HFOs)
* **Sub-bands:**
  * **Ripples ($80 - 250\text{ Hz}$):** Can be physiological (hippocampal memory consolidation) or pathological.
  * **Fast Ripples ($250 - 500\text{ Hz}$):** Exclusively pathological. Generated by small clusters of pathologically interconnected pyramidal neurons firing synchronously.
* **Presurgical Significance:** In intracranial EEG (iEEG/sEEG), channels demonstrating high rates of pre-ictal fast ripples define the **Epileptogenic Zone (EZ)**. Surgical resection of the EZ correlates strongly with seizure-free outcomes (Engel Class I).

### 4.3 Phase-Amplitude Coupling (PAC)
* Quantifies the interaction where the phase of low-frequency oscillations (e.g., delta $0.5 - 4\text{ Hz}$ or theta $4 - 8\text{ Hz}$) modulates the amplitude envelope of high-frequency activity (gamma $30 - 80\text{ Hz}$ or HFOs).
* PAC surges dramatically in the epileptogenic network during the pre-ictal transition window ($5 - 30$ minutes prior to electrographic onset), serving as an early forecasting biomarker.

### 4.4 Focal and Diffuse Background Abnormalities
* **Polymorphic Delta Activity (PDA):** Continuous, irregular slow waves localized to a specific brain lobe, indicating structural lesions (e.g., focal cortical dysplasia, tumor, hippocampal sclerosis).
* **Periodic Discharges:**
  * **Lateralized Periodic Discharges (LPDs / PLEDs):** Sharp wave complexes recurring at regular intervals ($0.5 - 2\text{ Hz}$) over one hemisphere, frequently seen in acute stroke, herpes simplex encephalitis, or status epilepticus.
  * **Generalized Periodic Discharges (GPDs):** Bilaterally synchronous discharges seen in severe anoxic encephalopathy or toxic-metabolic coma.
